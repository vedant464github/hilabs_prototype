Case Management Workbench: text version of the prototype

This is a plain-text description of every screen in the clickable prototype, for readers and tools that do not run JavaScript. In a browser the interactive app replaces this text. All members, IDs, phone numbers and providers are fictional.

Live prototype: https://vedant464github.github.io/hilabs_prototype/. Code, README with screenshots, and all prototype data as JSON: https://github.com/vedant464github/hilabs_prototype (data file: data.json).

Built for the HiLabs product caselet 2026-27 by Vedant Abhangrao, IIT Bombay. The product is a web workbench for a health plan's nurse case managers. The persona, Maria, has 48 cases and manages about two real conversations a day because her time goes to finding data across six or seven systems, dialling dead numbers and retyping notes. The prototype assumes HiLabs MCheck underneath: MCheck Clinical standardizes clinical data and MCheck Directory Accuracy checks provider details.

Screens

  1. Today: ranks members with new signals using readable rules, shows why, and plans each call for when the person can answer.
  2. Member 360: one page per member with deadlines, a cited summary of what changed, medicines by source with conflicts, next actions, timeline, open loops, quality gaps, social needs and source freshness.
  3. Call and note: ranked contacts, one-click outcomes, a call agenda, and AI-drafted note fields the nurse must confirm before signing once.
  4. Team lead: caseload against capacity and routing suggestions.
  5. About: design principles, what is automated and what the nurse decides, and a before and after of preparing one call.

About: principles and safeguards

  • Fast sources first: hospital alerts and pharmacy fills arrive in hours, claims in 30 to 90 days, so claims are used only for history.
  • Conflicts are shown side by side and never settled automatically.
  • Every fact carries a source and a date; stale data is labelled; any record can be reported wrong, which sends a data quality ticket to MCheck.
  • The queue runs on rules anyone can read; a predictive model may add to them after the pilot.
  • Automated: merging duplicate alerts, linking records at 95% match or above, extracting medicine changes and barriers from documents with quotes, cited summaries, pre-filling citable note fields, ranking numbers and logging outcomes. The nurse decides: matches below 95%, the medicine list and clinical advice, what to do, safety, observations and goals, who to call and what to say.
  • Left out on purpose: a predictive model in the ranking, automated calls or texts to members, a member app, home monitoring devices (later pilot).
  • Before and after for Lloyd: preparing the call takes about 16 minutes across seven systems today and about 6 minutes on one screen, about 99 minutes back per day across roughly 10 members. The time model moves real conversations from about 2 to about 5 or 6 a day.
